# Literature search and source-access record

Search date: **9 October 2026, America/Los_Angeles**. Search strings below were submitted for this study, followed by targeted primary-source inspection and bibliographic checks. This is a reconstructible record of a targeted literature study, not a preregistered systematic review; no exhaustive coverage or search-result denominator is claimed.

## Scope and screening rule

The object of the search was the reference architecture: separate development, operation, and improvement responsibilities; shared versioned semantics; evidence-driven candidate changes; dependent capability development; independently controlled promotion; semantic authority impact; and reproducible deployment/rollback contracts. Search terminology was broadened beyond “orchestrator” to established autonomic, adaptation, ontology-evolution, and release-governance terminology.

Primary papers, author-hosted manuscripts, institutional author records, standards, and project documentation were retained when they anticipated an architectural responsibility or materially constrained a novelty claim. Publisher/institutional abstracts were usable for their explicit positive claims. Uninspected mechanisms were never coded as absent. Blog posts and secondary summaries served only as discovery leads. Living project documentation was distinguished from dated research because its first-publication chronology was not established.

## Result and residual uncertainty

The broad thesis is substantially anticipated. The strongest combined challenge is older MAPE-K/ontology evolution plus **EvoOntology**, **Governed Capability Evolution**, and the **Jørgensen/Ma** authority framework. The supported position is architectural synthesis and an integrated reference contract, with exact-combination originality **unestablished**. The [contribution assessment](contribution-assessment.md) describes these boundaries and concrete falsification tests.

The study did not provide comprehensive ACM/IEEE/Scopus/Web of Science coverage, a patent search, an independent code/reproduction exercise, or an exhaustive citation-forward/backward traversal. Not every returned result was archived. Search engines can miss papers and recently revised preprints. Publication chronology of living documents requires archived snapshots or commit investigation before any priority argument. A future systematic review could broaden those dimensions, but this targeted study already supplies sufficient counterexamples to reject first-of-kind claims.
